### Synthesis of New Findings (500 words)

The 2024-2026 literature on organizational unlearning reveals three significant developments that advance the field beyond its foundational Hedberg-Becker lineage.

**First, unlearning is increasingly theorized as a dynamic process rather than a discrete event.** Klammer et al. (2024) provide the most comprehensive process-oriented framework to date, showing that organizational unlearning unfolds over time through interconnected phases of recognition, disengagement, and reconstruction. This process view is complemented by Maccioni et al.'s (2024) taxonomy, which categorizes unlearning types by depth and scope. Together, these works move the field past the binary "unlearn or not" framing toward nuanced understanding of how unlearning actually happens within organizations. Kociatkiewicz and Kostera (2025) add a reflexive dimension, arguing that forgetting is not merely learning's byproduct but its necessary twin -- strategic forgetting prevents the reproduction of organizational pathologies.

**Second, organizational unlearning is now firmly linked to digital transformation capability.** Multiple studies (Zhang et al., 2024; Ranjan & Narwal, 2025; Yang & Zhu, 2025) demonstrate empirically that organizational unlearning mediates the relationship between leadership cognition and digital transformation competency. Zhang et al.'s finding that top management team cognitive conflict drives unlearning, which in turn enables digital mastery, is particularly relevant to AI adoption contexts: it suggests that productive disagreement about existing assumptions is a precondition for successful technology integration, not an obstacle to it. Ranjan and Narwal (2025) extend this to emerging economies, showing that organizational agility and unlearning jointly predict innovation performance during digital transformation.

**Third, the field is expanding its conceptual boundaries.** Van Oers (2024) applies unlearning to sustainability transitions, defining it as discarding not just routines but entire worldviews that perpetuate unsustainability. Chen (2024) connects active organizational forgetting to ambidextrous innovation in high-tech firms, showing that deliberate forgetting of outdated knowledge is positively associated with both exploitative and exploratory innovation. Frie et al. (2024) introduce "flexpertise" as a construct describing how professionals sustain expertise relevance through continuous adaptation -- a construct that implicitly requires unlearning as domain boundaries shift. Murphy (2024) brings cognitive precision by framing "responsible forgetting" as strategic information prioritization, bridging individual cognition and organizational knowledge management.

**For AI/technology education design, four implications emerge.** First, unlearning programs should be structured as multi-phase processes with explicit recognition, confrontation, and reconstruction stages -- not as single-event interventions. Second, cognitive conflict at the leadership level is productive for digital transformation when channeled through unlearning processes, suggesting that education should deliberately induce and scaffold such conflicts. Third, the depth of unlearning matters: routine unlearning (changing habits) requires different pedagogical strategies than deep unlearning (challenging worldviews and value premises). Fourth, the concept of flexpertise provides a positive framing for professionals undergoing unlearning -- they are not losing expertise but adapting it to new domains and contexts.

The field still lacks longitudinal studies of unlearning in AI adoption specifically, and most empirical work focuses on manufacturing and supply chain contexts rather than software engineering or DevOps teams. This represents an important gap for curriculum development targeting AI Agent and platform adoption.

### Narrative Summary (300 words)

Recent cognitive science research (2024-2026) substantially strengthens the empirical foundations for unlearning-based educational design.

**Proactive interference in experts** is now better understood as context-dependent. Loaiza et al. (2025) demonstrate that expertise-driven working memory advantages collapse under high proactive interference conditions, meaning that the very knowledge base that makes someone an expert can actively sabotage new learning when old and new information compete. Pedraza et al. (2025) show that overcoming this interference requires active executive function engagement -- specifically inhibition and updating -- not merely exposure to new information. This finding directly supports the pedagogical strategy of explicitly training learners to suppress old mental models rather than simply presenting new ones.

**Memory reconsolidation** research has progressed from laboratory curiosity toward clinical application. Raut et al. (2024) demonstrate the first clinically viable pharmacological approach to disrupting reconsolidated fear memories in PTSD. Nadel and Sederberg's (2024) comprehensive handbook chapter confirms that reconsolidation operates across memory types -- fear, motor, and episodic -- validating its relevance to professional knowledge revision. The practical implication is clear: reactivating old knowledge (rather than ignoring it) opens a modification window where that knowledge can be updated.

**Automation bias** research reveals that the cognitive mechanisms behind silent failures in AI-assisted workflows operate through trust dynamics and verification fatigue. Romeo and Conti (2025) find that explainable AI alone is insufficient; Wingerter et al. (2025) show that simple UI nudges can nearly double performance when AI provides faulty outputs. Tian and Zhang (2025) identify cognitive fatigue as a mediator of AI dependence, with AI literacy serving as a protective buffer. These findings converge on a crucial design principle: reducing cognitive load through AI assistance paradoxically increases the risk of uncritical acceptance unless metacognitive scaffolding is deliberately embedded (Xu et al., 2025). For unlearning-oriented curricula, this means that the "ease" of AI-assisted workflows must be counterbalanced by structured verification habits and cognitive engagement protocols.

**첫째, AI 리터러시 교육의 체계화가 급속히 진행되고 있다.** Chee 등(2024)은 K-12부터 성인까지 아우르는 AI 리터러시 역량 프레임워크를 제시했고, Yang 등(2025)은 한국 K-12 교사 1,172명을 대상으로 AI 리터러시 자기인식의 핵심 요인을 머신러닝 기법으로 분석했다. 이 연구들은 한국에서 AI 교육이 단순한 기술 교육에서 체계적 역량 개발로 전환되고 있음을 보여준다. 특히 Kim 등(2025, 한국교원대)은 구성주의 원리에 기반한 AI 교육 데이터셋을 개발하여 학습자 맥락에 맞는 교육 자료의 중요성을 실증했다.

**둘째, 생성형 AI 교육에서 비판적 사고와 과의존 방지가 핵심 과제로 부상하고 있다.** Jang(2024)은 AI 리터러시가 GenAI 사용 의도뿐 아니라 비판적 활용 태도에도 영향을 미침을 발견했고, Lee 등(2024)은 AI 출력물의 불일치성이 오히려 사용자의 정보 이해도를 높일 수 있다는 역설적 결과를 보고했다. 이는 기존 보고서에서 다룬 automation bias와 직결되는 발견으로, 한국 맥락에서의 과의존 방지 교육 설계에 직접적 시사점을 제공한다.

**셋째, 인지과학적 근거의 한국화 작업이 시작되고 있다.** Yu와 Yu(2024)의 기억 재응고화 한국 상담 문헌 고찰은 재응고화 개념이 이미 한국 실무 현장에서 활용되고 있음을 확인해 주며, 교육적 언러닝의 인지과학적 근거를 한국 맥락에서 검증할 수 있는 기반을 마련했다. 다만 "언러닝(unlearning)" 자체를 교육학적 주제로 다룬 한국어 학술 논문은 아직 매우 제한적이며, 대부분의 관련 연구가 AI 리터러시, 개념변화, 인지갈등이라는 인접 개념을 통해 간접적으로 접근하고 있다. 이는 한국 교육 현장에서 언러닝 개념의 독자적 학술화가 아직 초기 단계에 있음을 시사하며, 본 커리큘럼이 이 공백을 메우는 선도적 역할을 할 수 있는 기회이기도 하다.

## Research Methodology Note

This literature review was conducted on 2026-04-19 using the OpenAlex API, Semantic Scholar API, and targeted web searches. Search terms included "organizational unlearning," "strategic forgetting," "knowledge renewal," "proactive interference expertise," "memory reconsolidation applications," "automation bias cognitive mechanisms," "cognitive flexibility training," and Korean-language equivalents. Papers were filtered for publication dates from 2024 onward. Machine unlearning papers (focused on removing training data influence from AI models) were systematically excluded. The OpenAlex database provided the primary source of structured bibliographic data, supplemented by Semantic Scholar for cognitive science literature.

### Key Limitations
- Korean-language databases (RISS, DBpia, KCI) were not directly accessible through API, limiting the Korean-language source coverage. The Korean sources listed here are primarily English-language papers by Korean researchers or Korean-affiliated institutions.
- The search for "언러닝" as a standalone Korean academic term yielded zero results in international databases, suggesting that the term has not yet been widely adopted in Korean-language academic literature.
- Some 2025-2026 papers may still be in press or behind paywalls, meaning additional relevant sources may exist beyond what is captured here.
